Speed is the number everyone chases. Accuracy is the number that decides how fast you actually work.

## The real cost of an error

An error is not one mistake. It is a sequence: you type the wrong character, your brain notices a fraction of a second later, you stop, you find backspace, you delete, you retype, and then you rebuild your place in the sentence.

That sequence takes roughly two to three seconds — during which a typist at 60 WPM would have typed three more words. Ten errors in a minute do not cost you ten characters; they cost you most of a minute.

This is why the arithmetic favours accuracy so heavily. At 99% accuracy, a 60 WPM typist finishes ahead of a 90 WPM typist at 90% accuracy on any real piece of writing.

## What your accuracy score is telling you

A single accuracy percentage hides the useful detail. What matters is *which* errors you make.

**Random errors** scattered across different keys usually mean you are typing above your controlled speed. The fix is straightforward: slow down until they stop.

**Repeated errors on the same keys** mean a specific reach was never learned properly. No amount of general practice fixes this — the key barely appears in normal text. It needs targeted drilling.

**Errors that cluster at the end of a session** are fatigue. That is a signal about session length or posture rather than technique.

**Transposition errors** — typing `teh` for `the`, `adn` for `and` — are a timing problem between hands, not a knowledge problem. They happen when one hand consistently fires slightly early.

## The habits that fix accuracy

**Stop looking at the screen for confirmation.** Many typists glance up after every few words to check their work. This breaks rhythm and, ironically, causes more errors than it catches. Type the sentence, then look.

**Keep your hands anchored.** If your hands drift from the home row, every reach becomes an estimate. The index fingers should find F and J by feel, and return to them.

**Slow down at the hard parts, not uniformly.** Skilled typists are not uniformly fast — they speed up on familiar patterns and slow down on awkward ones. Trying to hold one constant speed across everything guarantees errors on the difficult sequences.

**Do not correct mid-word by instinct.** Decide deliberately. Reflexive backspacing on every small mistake builds a stop-start rhythm that costs more than the errors do.

## The 97% rule

Practise at whatever speed keeps you above 97% accuracy. When you can hold that comfortably for a full session, push your speed up slightly and let accuracy dip to around 95%. Work there until it climbs back to 97%, then push again.

This is the whole method. It sounds too simple to be a technique, but it is the difference between practice that compounds and practice that just burns time.

## A note on blind mode

Some typists practise with correctness feedback hidden, seeing errors only at the end. This is worth trying once you are reasonably accurate — it stops you relying on colour cues and forces you to *feel* whether a keystroke was right.

It is a poor starting point, though. Without feedback, a beginner practises the same error for a full minute before finding out.

## Where to start

Take an honest measurement first — the [typing test](/typing-test/) reports accuracy alongside speed, and the gap between your raw and net WPM tells you exactly how much speed your errors are costing.

If your errors cluster on symbols or punctuation, the [symbol typing practice](/symbols-typing/) targets those reaches directly. For structured work with accuracy targets built in, the [typing lessons](/typing-lessons/) are the better path.
